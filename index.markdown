      <p>I am an assistant professor in the Department of Mathematics at <a href="https://math.uccs.edu/pax-kivimae" target="_blank" rel="noopener">UCCS</a>.</p>
      <p>My research lies primarily in probability theory, statistical physics, and non-convex optimization. In particular, I study high-dimensional random landscapes and disordered systems, including spin glasses, random matrices, and random media.</p>
      <p>These models originated in physics, but the same mathematics now appears in machine learning, high-dimensional statistics, and biology, where the geometry of a random landscape determines not only how algorithms move through it but also which solutions they find. I am especially interested in universal results, and in understanding the structure of those solutions: what is typical, what is rare, and how both change as the system grows.</p>
      <p>Before coming to Colorado, I was an NSF Postdoctoral Fellow at the Courant Institute at NYU, working with <a href="https://cims.nyu.edu/~benarous/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Gérard Ben Arous</a>. Even before that, I received my Ph.D. in mathematics from Northwestern University, where I was supervised by <a href="https://sites.math.northwestern.edu/~auffing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Antonio Auffinger</a>.</p>
